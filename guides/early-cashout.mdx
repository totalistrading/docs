You do not have to wait for settlement. Once a parlay is live on chain, you can ask market makers to
buy you out. Totalis runs a short auction, you see the best price live, and you accept it or walk
away.

## At a glance

- Any live parlay can be cashed out early, up to 30 seconds before it expires. The auction runs about 10 seconds.
- Every market maker sees it and bids one all-in price. You are shown the best one as it changes.
- You are only ever paid a price you explicitly accepted. If the price moves before you commit, nothing happens.
- The price can be below your stake if the parlay has gone against you.

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Request a cashout">
    Ask for a cashout on a live position. Totalis opens the auction and gives you a stream to watch it.

    <Accordion title="The call">
    ```bash
    POST /v1/cashout-requests
    { "position_id": "<position-id>" }
    GET  /v1/cashout-requests/<id>/stream   # SSE: live buyback quote and status
    ```
    `position_id` is on the parlay object once it is live.
    </Accordion>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Market makers bid">
    Every maker sees the auction and can bid one all-in price. The best live bid is what you see, and
    it can change until you accept.

    <Accordion title="On the wire">
    The [cashout stream](/api-reference/quote-service/cashout-stream) carries the best live bid. Its
    `price_micro` is the total you would receive, in microUSDC.
    </Accordion>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Accept the price">
    Take the best bid, or walk away. You are only ever filled at the price you accepted.

    <Accordion title="The call">
    ```bash
    POST /v1/cashout-requests/<id>/commit
    { "min_price_micro": <the price_micro you accepted> }
    ```
    `min_price_micro` is required. If the best bid has dropped below it, the commit fails and nothing
    happens.
    </Accordion>
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What the price means

The bid you accept is a single all-in figure, and exactly that amount lands in your vault as free
balance, not in your wallet. Trade with it or withdraw it from there. There is no separate fee line
and no second taker fee for leaving: the profit fee is already inside the number. See
[Fees on an early exit](/guides/limits-and-fees#fees-on-an-early-exit).

On a closing exit your stake is released and the difference between the price and your stake moves
between you and the maker. That difference, before the profit fee, is the `amount` on the
`position.bought_back` webhook, and `mm_pays_user` gives its direction.

The price can be below your stake. A parlay that has gone against you exits for less than it cost,
and you never pay more than your stake to leave.

## What happens to the position

An auction resolves one of two ways. Both pay you the same. They look different in your data.

| Winning bidder | What happens | How it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The maker who backs your position | The position closes. | The parlay reads as bought back, with realized P&L on its `cashout` object. |
| Any other maker | They take over the position, which stays open under their name. You are paid the same. | The parlay stays live in your reads. |

<Note>
If you build on the API, do not wait for a bought back status to know a cashout succeeded. Use the
commit response, or the `position.bought_back` webhook when the backing maker won. Market makers
have their own page for the other side of the auction: [Cashout auctions](/guides/cashout-auctions).
</Note>
